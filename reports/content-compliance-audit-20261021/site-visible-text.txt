Career Outlook For Registered Nurses

Registered nurses are the backbone of the healthcare industry. With a growing population, increasing rates of chronic conditions, and a greater need for preventative care, the demand for registered nurses will only increase.

According to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, the median annual wage for registered nurses was $77,600 in May 2021 and is expected to grow.

Over the next decade, registered nurse employment is projected to grow 9 percent from 2020 to 2030, about as fast as the average for all occupations. About 194,500 openings for registered nurses are projected each year, on average, over the decade. Many of those openings are expected to result from the need to replace workers who transfer to different occupations or exit the labor force, such as retiring.

About SABER College

Spanish American Basic Education and Rehabilitation, Inc.

Helping Students Since 1972

Spanish American Basic Education & Rehabilitation, Inc. (SABER) was founded in 1972 in Miami, Florida, to provide basic skills, language, vocational, and career education in high-demand occupational areas. SABER College operates under Spanish American Basic Education and Rehabilitation, Inc. (SABER), doing business as SABER College—a private, not-for-profit corporation formed under the laws of the State of Florida and recognized as a 501(c)(3) organization. For the past 38 years, it has been led by the same executive administration and is widely respected for its contributions to workforce development in the Miami area. If you’d like to learn more about SABER College, our mission, or our programs, please don’t hesitate to contact us or visit our Contact Page.

SABER Philosophy

The philosophy of SABER College is to act as a vehicle to provide basic skills, language, vocational, and career education in various high demand occupational areas. It is the purpose of SABER College to contribute to the economic growth and development of the local community through training programs and activities that help to improve job skills, secure/retain/create better vocational and career opportunities for its residents, as well as generate new vocational and career opportunities for those students who apply themselves.

SABER College’s goal is to improve the students’ quality of life in the community and facilitate career opportunities in the marketplace and industry.

Mission

SABER College identifies as its primary responsibility the preparation of individuals to become productive members of this society by helping them in their growth to attain educational goals and their development of ethical responsibilities.

SABER History

Spanish American Basic Education & Rehabilitation, Inc. (SABER) was established in 1972 in Florida to provide knowledge, skills, abilities and trained students due to a dire demand in the community for individuals in the various occupational fields. SABER College was established to offer training through non-degree and vocational and degree granting programs to meet the demands of the industry and the workforce community in general; SABER College has been licensed by the Commission for Independent Education (CIE) since 1991 and is accredited by the Council on Occupational Education (COE).

SABER Ownership

Spanish American Basic Education and Rehabilitation, Inc. (SABER) dba SABER College, 3990 W. Flagler Street, Miami, Florida 33134 is a private non-profit corporation formed under the laws of the State of Florida, and which adheres to IRS regulations as a 501 (c)(3) corporation.

Safety Plan - COE Health and Safety Plan
SABER College’s General Campus Safety and Recovery Plan prioritizes a secure, compliant environment per the Clery Act. In emergencies, protocols are activated to safeguard students, staff, and guests, with clear guidelines for accident response, reporting, and investigation. Accreditation
Accreditation Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is SABER College accredited?

A: Yes. SABER College is accredited by the Council on Occupational Education (COE) and has been licensed by the Florida Department of Education’s Commission for Independent Education (CIE) since 1992 (License #1400). SABER College is a private, non-profit 501(c)(3) institution.

Q: Is the Professional Nursing Program accredited?

A: Yes. The SABER College Associate Degree in the Nursing Program holds accreditation from the National League for Nursing Commission for Nursing Education Accreditation (NLN CNEA), 2600 Virginia Avenue, NW, Washington, DC 20037; (202) 909-2487. Graduates are eligible to sit for the NCLEX-RN.

Q: Is the Physical Therapist Assistant (PTA) Program accredited?

A: Yes. The PTA Program is accredited by the Commission on Accreditation in Physical Therapy Education (CAPTE), 3030 Potomac Ave., Suite 100, Alexandria, VA 22305-3085; (703) 706-3245; accreditation@apta.org; www.capteonline.org. Graduates are eligible to sit for the National Physical Therapy Examination (NPTE) for PTAs.

Q: Why does accreditation matter?

A: Accreditation means SABER College meets recognized national and state standards for quality education. It supports eligibility for federal financial aid (for those who qualify), the ability to sit for licensure examinations, and the value of your degree with employers.

Q: How can I verify SABER College’s accreditation?

A: You can confirm our accreditation directly with each agency: COE (www.council.org), CIE (www.fldoe.org/policy/cie), CAPTE (www.capteonline.org), and NLN CNEA (cnea.nln.org).

Nursing A.S. Degree Program Overview

When you decide to become a nurse, you choose to change lives, including your own. In our registered nursing program, you’ll develop the skills you need for a career in caregiving. The degree program will allow you to take charge of your future and practice in a safe, supportive, and stimulating environment. The ASN program is designed to help registered nurses (RN) improve their skills through didactic and clinical experiences that prepare them for various roles in nursing practice.

There are a lot of accelerated nursing programs out there, and it can be hard to determine the right fit. At SABER College of Nursing, we understand that the future of healthcare relies on nurses who are educated in evidence-based practice. We pride ourselves on our small class sizes, which allow for individualized attention from faculty and students. This attention translates into helping you potentially attain workplace readiness faster.

What Do You Learn In Nursing School?

Before starting to work as a registered nurse, it is wise to review some of the core concepts you will need to know to provide safe and effective patient care. These include anatomy and physiology, nutrition and diet, microbiology and nursing pharmacology, and lessons on how drugs work in the body.

It’s also essential for you to build a network of peers in your classes. When exam time rolls around, you can study together and help each other with any confusing concepts. If a group study session isn’t your jam, try getting a tutor or study buddy who can help you review critical concepts before your exams.

Finally, remember that feeling overwhelmed is normal! Talk with friends who have been through nursing school before you. Successful students can tell you what it was like for them and how they coped with challenging classes and stressful assignments.

What Do Professional Nurses (RNs) Do?

Nurses offer various services to patients in a hospital or clinic setting. They perform physical exams and take detailed health care histories. They are also the first to run blood, urine, and other tests, administer medications ordered by physicians, and treat injuries. Nurses may also be called upon to chart a patient’s progress, counsel patients on diet changes or lifestyle modifications, and assist doctors during surgery.

The key responsibilities of a nurse are:

Being the primary point of contact for patients and their families
Providing a wide range of health care services, including administering medication, operating medical equipment, dressing wounds, and recording patient information
Maintaining accurate and complete medical records.
Responding to emergencies when they occur and assisting in emergencies
Collaborating with other health care professionals to coordinate patient care plans and ensure quality patient outcomes
What It Means To Be A NCLEX RN

Being a nurse is an important job: you help people. But the NCLEX-RN offers the opportunity to do even more than that.

The NCLEX-RN serves as a guide for examination development and candidate preparation. The NCLEX® examination assesses the knowledge, skills, and abilities essential for the entry-level nurse to meet clients' needs requiring the promotion, maintenance, or restoration of health.

It's hard to be a nurse, but completing your NCLEX-RN is one way you can help make sure that you're ready for anything that comes your way.

What is the difference between ADN, AASN, and ASN nursing programs?

ADN, ASN, and AASN programs are all Associate degrees in nursing—but they're not the same.

The most crucial difference between them is that the Associate of Science in Nursing (ASN) focuses on preparing students for higher-level degrees. At the same time, the Associate Degree in Nursing (ADN) and the Associate of Applied Science in Nursing (AASN) focus on teaching students how to be entry-level nurses.

ASN and ADN nursing programs may prepare nurses more for clinical work than bachelor's-level programs.
AASN nursing programs may prepare nurses more for supervisory work within their field than ASN or ADN programs.

The main reason you'd want to get an ASN is if you think you might want to go on and get a Bachelor of Science in Nursing (BSN). With an ASN, you'll need fewer credits to complete a BSN than with an ADN or AASN—meaning less time and money spent getting your next degree.

In general, ADNs and AASNs are considered more flexible than ASNs. If you don't know that you want to go into nursing, or if you have other commitments that require a flexible schedule, this may be the path for you.

It's also worth noting that some employers prefer ADNs or AASNs because they consider them to be more directly related to their field than ASNs. It all depends on where you're applying.

PTA A.S. Degree Program Overview

Do you have a passion for helping others in need? Is it your dream to one day have a career that helps people improve their lives by overcoming physical challenges? If either of these things rings true, you should strongly consider a career in physical therapy and health sciences.

The SABER College Physical Therapist Assistant education program is a unique mix of theory, skills, and clinical training. The curriculum includes an in-depth study of anatomy and kinesiology, pathophysiology, patient data collection, and therapeutic intervention. After completing the Physical Therapist Assistant program, you will be prepared to provide care under licensed physical therapist supervision.

The Physical Therapist Assistant program (PTA) provides the opportunity for individuals to gain knowledge and experience in a medical setting. Graduates will be prepared to work with the physical therapist to provide physical therapy services, assistive devices, and daily living activities.

Program Related Job Training for PTAs

We believe that hands-on clinical training is the best way to learn to work with patients as a PTA. Our physical therapist assistant program provides job training and achieves just that. Hands-on will help you manage your expectations and become more efficient in your work. Training of this type will make you more prosperous and make your patients happier.

Hands-on job training may also teach you to handle different situations and circumstances. You should practice in different situations so that you're prepared for anything that might happen during your session with a client.

What Does a Physical Therapist Assistant Do?

Physical therapy assistants sometimes called PTAs, and physical therapist aides, work under the direction and supervision of physical therapists. They help patients recovering from injuries and illnesses regain movement and manage pain.

As a physical therapists assistant, you may be expected to perform the following duties:

Evaluate and treat patients under the direction of physical therapists.
May plan and implement specific treatment programs for individual patients according to the principles and practices of physical therapy.
Administer active and passive manual therapeutic exercises, therapeutic massage, and traction to help relieve pain, increase patient strength
Set up and operate devices such as traction equipment and electrotherapeutic apparatus which apply electricity, light, heat, cold or sound waves
Apply surgical dressings to protect injured areas during therapy sessions.
Clerical tasks like maintaining records on each patient treated by the physical therapist assistants.
Instruct patients on how to use their bodies properly while performing various tasks at home and at work
Career Outlook for PTAs

The United States Bureau of Labor Statistics expects employment for Physical Therapist Assistants and Aides to grow 32% from 2020 to 2030. This is much faster than the average for all occupations. Physical therapist assistant job growth in the physical therapy field is expected to be driven by the increasing number of individuals with mobility issues.

The Bureau estimates that about 23,800 openings for Physical Therapist Assistants and Aides are projected each year, on average, over the decade. Many job openings are expected to come from workers who leave their current occupation or retire.

A physical therapist assistant's salary was $61,180.1 (median) in 2021 as reported by the BLS. It’s important to note that the PTA salary will vary based on experience and employer. The lowest 10 percent earned less than $37,280, and the highest 10 percent earned more than $80,170.1

CAPTE is the only accrediting agency recognized by the U.S. Department of Education for physical therapy education. We are a SEVP-SEVIS Certified School
As a school, we are SEVP-certified by the Student and Exchange Visitor Program, administered by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) from the Department Of Homeland Security. The program helps ensure that United States schools comply with the full range of requirements for international students.
To be a SEVP-certified school, we must undergo rigorous oversight of all aspects of the program, including immigration compliance and international student services. This means that our school is approved to enroll students with an F-1 student visa and M-1 international students with an I-20, which is a document they need to apply for a student visa.
I-20 Process - International student services
As an international student, you’ll need to complete the following steps:
Complete all academic requirements for admission to a degree program. This step is handled exclusively by the SABER Admissions Office for students pursuing an associate’s degree program or general education classes.
Prepare proof of financial support. The International Student Services office will need your I-20 Form, which lists your financial resources and course of study. The SABER Admissions Office will issue this form.
As part of your I-20 application, you must submit a completed affidavit of support form with a bank signature or bank letter detailing funding, a government sponsorship letter, or any combination thereof to your I-20 application.Note: Government sponsorship letters must specifically mention SABER College as the designated school for the student, and government sponsorship letters must cover one full year (12 months) of student costs if used as the sole source of funding.
To provide you with your I-20, SABER’s International Student Services department will need a scanned copy of your passport’s data page. This should include a clear image of your name, date of birth, and passport expiration date. Note that F-1 visa holders must maintain a passport that is at least 6 months from expiration while inside the United States.
To provide you with your I-20, SABER’s International Student Services department will need a scanned copy of your passport’s data page. This should include a clear image of your name, date of birth, and passport expiration date. Note that F-1 visa holders must maintain a passport that is at least 6 months from expiration while inside the United States.
If you are bringing any of your underage children or spouse to the U.S. with you, we need to know so that we can add them to your I-20 application as F-2 dependents. If your dependents are arriving in the U.S. after you have received your I-20, please you will need to complete the “Add New Dependent” form instead.
Once you have completed all five steps and submitted them to our office, we will begin processing your I-20. You will receive an e-mail message asking you to confirm that all the data on your I-20 is accurate. Be sure to double-check details such as the spelling of your name and the accuracy of your date of birth.
You will also be required to provide information for shipping your form I-20.

Net Price Calculator

The Net Price Calculator at SABER College is an essential tool that helps prospective students and their families estimate the cost of attending college. Understanding tuition, fees, and potential financial aid is one of the most important steps in planning for your education. SABER College is committed to transparency and accessibility, providing this federally required tool to support informed financial decision-making.

This calculator offers an early look at your estimated net cost—the difference between the total cost of attendance and any non-loan financial aid you may be eligible to receive. Using historical data and the information you provide, the tool delivers a personalized estimate tailored to your unique circumstances. While it does not replace the official financial aid process, it serves as a valuable resource for financial planning.
What Is the Net Price Calculator?
The Net Price Calculator is designed for undergraduate students exploring enrollment at SABER College. It allows you to estimate your likely education costs after grant and scholarship aid, based on a combination of federal formulas and previous year institutional data.
Though not an official financial offer, the results offer insight into what similar students paid in prior years—giving you a better sense of affordability before you complete your FAFSA or submit an application. This is just one of the ways SABER College supports students in preparing for higher education.
Why Use the Net Price Calculator?
Selecting the right college is a significant decision—both personally and financially. The Net Price Calculator gives you the clarity needed to:
Understand possible out-of-pocket expenses
Preview the impact of financial aid packages
Begin planning realistically for your educational investment
This tool is particularly useful for those pursuing Gainful Employment programs at SABER College, including our Nursing and Physical Therapist Assistant programs.
Whether you’re a recent high school graduate or a working adult returning to school, this resource helps you make a more confident and informed choice.
How to Use It
Using the calculator is quick and straightforward. In about 5–10 minutes, you’ll answer a few basic questions about income, household size, and dependency status. Based on your answers, the tool will generate an estimate of your net price—the cost of attendance minus average financial aid.
Keep in mind, the more accurate the information you enter, the more closely your estimate will reflect your potential financial aid eligibility. While the calculator cannot predict every detail, it provides a useful baseline for budgeting and comparison.

SABER College Graduation Requirements
At SABER College, graduation requirements are carefully structured to ensure that every student who completes a degree in Professional Nursing or Physical Therapist Assistant is fully prepared to enter the healthcare workforce with the clinical skills, academic knowledge, and ethical standards required in today’s patient care environments.
SABER College offers two Associate of Science (A.S.) degree programs that are designed to develop competence in patient-centered, culturally sensitive care. Students are trained to recognize the relationship between the individual needs of patients and the broader needs of society. To ensure that all graduates meet these standards, the College has established specific academic, attendance, and financial requirements that must be fulfilled in order to earn a diploma and pursue licensure or further study.
Core Graduation Requirements
To be eligible for graduation from SABER College, students enrolled in either the Professional Nursing or Physical Therapist Assistant program must meet the following institutional standards:
1. Passing Grades in All Required Courses

Students must successfully complete all required coursework with a passing grade, including classroom, laboratory, and clinical components. A passing grade indicates that the student has demonstrated competence in the subject matter and is capable of applying theoretical knowledge in practical settings.

2. Completion of Required Instructional Hours
All students are required to meet a minimum 80% attendance rate across the total instructional hours in their program. This includes attendance for lectures, labs, and clinical experiences. Meeting this threshold ensures students receive the full benefit of instruction and hands-on learning opportunities essential to healthcare training.
3. Fulfillment of All Financial Obligations
Students must fulfill all monetary obligations to SABER College prior to graduation. This includes paying tuition, fees, and any other outstanding balances. Students with unresolved financial accounts will not receive official transcripts, diplomas, or documentation of program completion until their accounts are settled in full.
Program-Specific Expectations
In addition to the general requirements listed above, each program may include additional graduation requirements that reflect its curriculum and regulatory standards. These may include final evaluations, capstone assessments, or competency check-offs that are necessary for confirming a student’s readiness to graduate and enter the profession.
These program-specific expectations are outlined in the official student handbooks distributed at the time of enrollment. Students are responsible for reviewing their handbook and ensuring they meet all listed criteria. For personalized guidance, students are encouraged to consult with their academic advisors or program directors.
Supporting Student Readiness
SABER College is committed to graduating students who are not only academically qualified, but also prepared to deliver healthcare that is ethical, effective, and responsive to diverse patient populations. The graduation requirements are designed to uphold this standard while giving students a clear, structured path toward degree completion.
By meeting these requirements, graduates demonstrate that they are capable of providing culturally competent, patient-centered care—a core value of SABER College’s mission and a critical expectation in modern healthcare professions.
Next Steps and Support
If you are a current student approaching graduation or planning your academic pathway, SABER College offers advising and support services to help you stay on track. Our academic and financial aid teams are available to answer questions and provide guidance throughout the process.
For detailed information regarding eligibility, required coursework, and program-specific criteria, please consult your official program handbook or speak with your program director.

Gainful Employment Programs at SABER College
SABER College offers federally recognized Gainful Employment programs designed to prepare students for careers in nursing, physical therapy, and allied health fields. In accordance with U.S. Department of Education regulations, institutions like SABER College are required to disclose specific facts about these programs to help prospective students make informed decisions about their education and career path.
These disclosures include details such as program duration, occupational outcomes, typical student loan debt, and graduation or job placement data. They are intended to ensure that students understand both the academic commitment and career possibilities associated with each program before enrolling.
Under 34 CFR § 668.6, the federal government requires institutions of higher education to share program-level facts for all eligible gainful employment programs. These disclosures provide answers to key questions such as:
How long does the program take to complete?
What types of careers can I pursue upon graduation?
What is the typical loan debt for program graduates?
What are the job placement and completion outcomes?
This level of transparency empowers students to compare similar programs across colleges and choose the option that best fits their personal goals and financial situation.
Gainful Employment Programs at SABER College
The following Associate of Science degree programs at SABER College are designated as gainful employment programs:
1. Associate of Science – Physical Therapist Assistant (PTA)
This program prepares students to assist licensed physical therapists in delivering rehabilitative care. Graduates are equipped to work in hospitals, outpatient centers, and long-term care facilities.
2. Associate of Science – Professional Nursing Program (PNP)
SABER College’s Nursing program prepares students to sit for the NCLEX-RN and begin professional practice as Registered Nurses. Graduates work in a wide range of healthcare environments including hospitals, clinics, and community care centers.

SABER College Distance Education offers students the flexibility to begin or complete coursework online while maintaining the quality, structure, and support that define our academic programs. Distance education is not a separate program or credential—it is a delivery modality, meaning a method of instructional delivery. At SABER College, distance education is used strategically to expand access and provide flexible learning options for students pursuing healthcare careers.
As part of our commitment to accessible, career-driven education, SABER College offers select courses and program components via distance education. This includes the first semester of our accredited healthcare programs, including Professional Nursing and Physical Therapist Assistant (PTA). After this initial online phase, healthcare students transition to on-campus instruction for the hands-on and clinical components of their training.
Whether you are preparing for licensure or beginning your healthcare education, SABER College Distance Education offers flexible entry points into high-quality academic pathways designed for real-world outcomes.
Online Start for Healthcare Programs
As part of our commitment to flexibility and student access, SABER College offers the first semester of the Nursing and PTA programs through distance education. Students can begin their healthcare education with the convenience of remote learning—while staying connected to instructors and academic support every step of the way.

From your first inquiry to program completion, you’ll have access to responsive staff and qualified faculty who are committed to helping you stay informed, confident, and connected.
